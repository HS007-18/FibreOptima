Textile manufacturing requires precise control over humidity, machine speed, and age. If a machine's age exceeds 10 years, its operating speed should not exceed 250 rpm. If operated beyond 250 rpm, the loom will vibrate excessively, leading to micro-tears in the fabric, generating massive amounts of waste even if production seems high. 
When humidity drops below 50%, natural fibers like Cotton and Wool become brittle and snap on the loom. A missing humidity reading usually means the sensor failed, and operators must assume extreme dryness. Running a machine without humidity control will spike waste percentage above 15%, triggering a High Risk scenario.
If an operator runs a batch with high absolute waste (e.g., 50 kg) but the production quantity is extremely high (e.g., 5000 kg), this is entirely normal. The waste percentage is only 1%. This is a Normal batch. Do not flag a batch simply because absolute waste quantity is high.
If production quantity is very low (e.g., 50 kg) but waste is 20 kg, the waste percentage is 40%. This is catastrophic and always a High Risk batch. The machine must be stopped and inspected immediately.
